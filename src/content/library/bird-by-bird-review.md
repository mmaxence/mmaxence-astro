## Design is storytelling

I often reference *Bird by Bird* because so much of what I take from it applies directly to design. It is a book about writing, but I read it with the same questions I bring to my work: what am I trying to say, who am I speaking to, and how can I help them understand?

Design depends on our capacity to present ideas and concepts to an audience. A concept needs a story people can follow: the situation, what matters, the choices we made, and what those choices make possible. That is as much part of the work as the interface itself.

## Find the story through the work

The “bird by bird” approach reminds me to start with something small enough to make concrete. One interaction, one example, one part of the argument. Giving it form helps me see what is missing and what deserves more attention.

I also find permission here to work through imperfect drafts. An early sketch or presentation does not need to carry the finished idea. It needs to make the thinking visible enough to question and improve.

## Why I keep coming back

For me, this book connects making with communicating. Presenting a design is another opportunity to clarify it: choosing what the audience needs to know, finding the right sequence, and removing what gets in the way. That is why a book about writing has a lasting place on my design shelf.
